# Item-Writing Style Guide — FOM Simulator Question Bank

Read this entirely before writing any item. Every item is checked against it.

## 0. Non-negotiables

1. **Original items only.** Never reproduce, paraphrase, or "lightly adapt" any NCBAHM/NCCAOM sample item, any published practice-exam item, or any textbook self-test question. Write from the *concepts*.
2. **One defensible answer.** Exactly one option is correct under the mainstream TCM references listed in `docs/EXAM-SPEC.md` §6 (Maciocia, Cheng Xinnong, Deng Tietao, Wiseman & Ellis, Maclean & Lyttleton). If two options are defensible under different schools, rewrite until only one is.
3. **No negatives.** Never use "NOT", "EXCEPT", "LEAST", "false", or "all of the following except". NCBAHM item writers are instructed to avoid these.
4. **No "all of the above" / "none of the above" / "A and C".**
5. **Four options**, labeled A–D, homogeneous in grammar and length. Options should be parallel (all patterns, or all treatment principles, or all pulse qualities). No option should be conspicuously longer or more detailed than the others.
6. **No trick questions, no giveaway wording.** The stem must not contain the answer's key term. Avoid absolute words ("always", "never") in options.
7. **No fabricated references.** Cite the book and a chapter/section/topic only if you are confident it exists. If unsure of the chapter, cite the book title alone. Never invent page numbers.
8. **Coherent vignettes.** Signs, symptoms, tongue, and pulse must all belong to the same pattern (or the specified combined pattern). No contradictory findings unless the item is explicitly about a combined/complex pattern and the findings are the textbook combination.
9. **Scope.** FOM content only: assessment methods, pattern differentiation, pathomechanisms, treatment principles/planning, self-care counseling, Chinese dietary therapy. **No** point locations, needling/moxa technique, safety/clean needle, herbal formulas or single herbs (a treatment *principle* like "tonify Spleen Qi and resolve Dampness" is in scope; a formula name is not). **No** biomedical diagnosis as the answer.

## 1. Terminology

- Maciocia/Cheng Xinnong register. Capitalize organ names and substances when used in the TCM sense: Liver, Spleen, Qi, Blood, Yin, Yang, Essence (Jing), Spirit (Shen), Body Fluids, Phlegm, Dampness, Heat, Cold, Wind, Dryness, Summer-Heat, Fire.
- Pattern names: "Liver Blood deficiency", "Spleen Qi deficiency", "Kidney Yang deficiency", "Liver Qi stagnation", "Liver Yang rising", "Liver Fire blazing", "Damp-Heat in the Spleen", "Phlegm-Heat harassing the Heart", "Wind-Cold invading the Lung", "Cold-Damp invading the Spleen", "Blood stasis", "Stomach Yin deficiency", "Heart Blood deficiency", "Kidney Essence deficiency", "Lung Qi deficiency", "Lung Yin deficiency", "Gallbladder Damp-Heat", "Bladder Damp-Heat", "Large Intestine Damp-Heat", "Small Intestine Qi pain", "Retention of Food in the Stomach".
- Pulse qualities: floating, deep, slow, rapid, empty (deficient), full (excess), thin (thready / fine), weak, wiry, slippery, choppy, tight, soggy (soft / floating-fine), hidden, hollow, leather, scattered, moving, hasty, knotted, intermittent, long, short, overflowing (flooding), minute (faint), firm, slowed-down, hurried. Give one alternate name in parentheses on first use when helpful, e.g. "thin (thready)".
- Tongue: body color (pale, red, deep red / crimson, purple / purplish, bluish), body shape (swollen, thin, teeth-marked, cracked, stiff, deviated, flaccid, trembling, short, long), coating (thin white, thick white, yellow, greasy / sticky, dry, peeled / mirror, rootless), sublingual veins (distended, dark).
- Pinyin, where used: Wu Xing, Ba Gang, Jing Luo, Zang Fu, Shen, Jing, Wei Qi, Ying Qi, Zong Qi, Yuan Qi, Gu Qi, San Jiao, Tai Yang, Yang Ming, Shao Yang, Tai Yin, Shao Yin, Jue Yin, Ben/Biao. No tone marks.

## 2. Stem patterns (match the official register)

- Theory recall: "According to Five Element theory, which emotion is associated with the Lung?"
- Assessment: "A pulse that feels like a taut guitar string under the fingers is best described as…"  /  "A tongue with a thick, greasy yellow coating most commonly indicates…"
- Inquiry: "A patient reports night sweats. Which follow-up question would best help differentiate Yin deficiency from Damp-Heat?"
- Vignette → diagnosis: "A 46-year-old patient reports [chief complaint] for [duration]. [3–6 associated symptoms]. The tongue is [body], with [coating]. The pulse is [quality]. Which of the following is the most appropriate pattern diagnosis?"
- Vignette → treatment principle: "…Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment principle?" Options are lowercase verb phrases: "tonify Spleen Qi and resolve Dampness".
- Priorities: "…Which of the following should be addressed first?"
- Dietary: "Which dietary recommendation is most appropriate for this patient?"
- Self-care/explanation: "Which explanation of the diagnosis is most appropriate to give this patient?"

Use "most appropriate", "best describes", "most consistent with", "most likely". Keep stems under ~70 words; vignettes under ~90 words.

## 3. Difficulty and cognitive level

| difficulty | cognitiveLevel | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| foundational | recall | A single correspondence, definition, or property. |
| foundational | comprehension | Explain *why* a sign points to a mechanism. |
| intermediate | application | Apply theory to a short clinical description; 2–3 findings. |
| intermediate | analysis | Full vignette with tongue + pulse; differentiate between 2–4 similar patterns. |
| advanced | analysis | Combined/complex patterns, root vs branch, sequencing, contradictory-looking findings explained by one mechanism. |
| advanced | evaluation | Choose the best priority or plan modification given patient response or progression. |

Per slice, aim for roughly 30% foundational / 45% intermediate / 25% advanced unless the slice brief says otherwise. **At least half** of Domain II and III items must be clinical vignettes.

## 4. Distractors

Each distractor must be the answer a learner with a *specific* misconception would pick. Name that misconception in the distractor explanation. Good distractor families:
- Yin deficiency vs Yang deficiency vs Qi deficiency vs Blood deficiency of the same organ.
- Blood deficiency vs Blood stasis.
- Liver Qi stagnation vs Liver Yang rising vs Liver Fire vs Liver Wind.
- Spleen Qi deficiency vs Spleen Yang deficiency vs Cold-Damp vs Damp-Heat.
- Kidney Yin vs Kidney Yang vs Kidney Essence deficiency.
- Wind-Cold vs Wind-Heat vs Wind-Damp (exterior).
- Full Heat vs Empty Heat; Full Cold vs Empty Cold.
- Generating vs Controlling vs Overacting vs Insulting sequence.
- Floating vs empty vs soggy; wiry vs tight; slippery vs soggy; thin vs weak; choppy vs knotted.
- Greasy vs dry vs peeled coating; red vs deep-red vs purple body.
- Tai Yang vs Shao Yang vs Yang Ming; Wei vs Qi vs Ying vs Xue level.
- Root-first vs branch-first vs simultaneous.

## 5. Explanations

- `explanation`: 2–5 sentences. State the pattern/mechanism, then tie **each** key finding in the stem to it. Where the item is a differentiation, state the single discriminating finding.
- `distractorExplanations`: one for each wrong option, 1–3 sentences, naming what finding(s) rule it out and the misconception that would lead to it.
- `learningObjective`: one sentence, starts with a verb ("Differentiate…", "Identify…", "Formulate…").
- `distinction` (optional but strongly encouraged for differentiation items): `{ "between": ["pattern-a-slug", "pattern-b-slug"], "key": "one sentence naming the discriminating finding" }`. This powers the app's "Explain the Distinction" feature.

## 6. JSON schema (one object per item; arrays per slice file)

```json
{
  "id": "FOM-IIf-0001",
  "domain": "II",
  "subdomain": "II-f",
  "task": "II-1-f",
  "theory": "Zang Fu",
  "stem": "A 38-year-old patient reports ...",
  "options": { "A": "...", "B": "...", "C": "...", "D": "..." },
  "correct": "B",
  "explanation": "...",
  "distractorExplanations": { "A": "...", "C": "...", "D": "..." },
  "learningObjective": "Differentiate ...",
  "difficulty": "intermediate",
  "cognitiveLevel": "analysis",
  "vignette": true,
  "concepts": ["liver-blood-deficiency", "liver-yin-deficiency"],
  "distinction": { "between": ["liver-blood-deficiency", "liver-yin-deficiency"], "key": "..." },
  "references": [
    { "source": "Maciocia, The Foundations of Chinese Medicine, 3rd ed.", "locator": "Chapter on Liver patterns" }
  ]
}
```

Field rules:
- `id`: `FOM-<subdomain without hyphen>-<4-digit>` using the slice's assigned number range. Subdomain codes: IA, IB, IC, ID, IIa…IIm, III1…III6.
- `task`: outline task statement ref. Domain I: `I-A-3` etc. Domain II: `II-1-<letter>` (a–m). Domain III: `III-1-<letter>` for principles by theory, else `III-2`, `III-3`, `III-4`, `III-5`, `III-6`.
- `theory` (Domain II/III-1 only): one of `Yin/Yang`, `Five Elements`, `Eight Principles`, `Qi Blood Fluids`, `Channels`, `Zang Fu`, `Six Stages`, `Four Levels`, `San Jiao`, `Internal etiology`, `External etiology`, `Misc etiology`, `Organ clock`.
- `concepts`: 1–4 kebab-case slugs from a shared vocabulary (pattern names, pulse qualities, tongue signs, theory concepts). Use the *canonical* slug for the pattern tested (e.g. `spleen-qi-deficiency`, `kidney-yang-deficiency`, `wiry-pulse`, `greasy-yellow-coating`, `generating-cycle`, `root-and-branch`).
- `difficulty`: `foundational` | `intermediate` | `advanced`. `cognitiveLevel`: `recall` | `comprehension` | `application` | `analysis` | `evaluation`.
- Correct answer position: distribute roughly evenly across A/B/C/D within each slice (the app also shuffles, but balance the source).

## 7. Self-check before submitting a slice

For every item, confirm: (1) single correct answer; (2) each distractor is wrong for a nameable reason; (3) tongue + pulse + symptoms coherent; (4) no negatives; (5) in FOM scope; (6) stem does not leak the answer; (7) not a near-duplicate of another item in the slice (vary organ, pattern, patient, chief complaint); (8) references real; (9) JSON valid.
